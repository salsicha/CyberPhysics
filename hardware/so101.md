# SO-101 station bill of materials

Goal: a physical SO-101 arm station that matches the CyberPhysics SO-101
simulation interface (`applications/so101`, `compositions/so101_hardware.yaml`)
and its picking scene (`systems/so101/scenarios/picking_table.json`): a
follower arm on a riser, a RealSense D435i on a post at the scene camera's
pose, the scene's bins and objects, and a leader arm to record
demonstrations with LeRobot.

The arm is TheRobotStudio's open SO-101 (SO-ARM100 repository, Apache-2.0):
its printed parts are vendored unchanged, pinned by commit and SHA-256. The
station around it (riser, camera post foot and head, camera cradle, cable
clips, task bins and objects, calibration board feet) is designed here in
CadQuery. Printing and assembly instructions:
[hardware/parts/so101/README.md](parts/so101/README.md).

## Compatibility notes

- **Leader arm.** The repository has no teleoperation code of its own, but
  every real-data path needs demonstrations recorded on this station: the
  base GR00T N1.7 checkpoint cannot control the SO-101 zero-shot and SmolVLA
  must be fine-tuned on SO-101 data (`applications/groot/README.md`,
  `applications/smolvla/README.md`), and LeRobot records them by
  teleoperating the follower from an SO-101 leader
  (`--teleop.type=so101_leader`). Build the leader with the follower. For
  scripted control or policy playback only, the follower alone is enough.
- **Depth camera: RealSense D435i, fixed over the table.** The scene's camera
  (`groot_camera`, the GR00T front view) is a fixed camera 0.47 m above and
  0.52 m to the right of the arm, and the simulators publish a camera IMU
  (`/so101/camera/imu`): that is the D435i (69.4 deg colour HFOV, IMU,
  min-Z 195 mm at 848 x 480). The D405 that the old list offered instead has
  no IMU and is made for 7-50 cm: it does not suit a camera 0.5-0.9 m from
  the objects. It only makes sense as an extra wrist camera (upstream has a
  D405 wrist mount), which this repository's front-only contract does not
  use (`wrist_camera_topic` is empty).
- **The simulated camera is the D435i's colour stream.** The scene's camera
  is 640 x 360 (16:9) with square pixels and a 69.4 deg horizontal view,
  so 42.5 deg vertically, as the D435i's colour sensor; the hardware
  compositions select the same mode (`rgb_camera.color_profile:=640x360x30`),
  so simulated and real frames have the same size and field of view. The
  scene's camera is pitched 48.5 deg down, the pitch the assembly check
  recommends (every bin and object within 88 % of the half-image) and the
  one the printed head is trimmed to; at the old 45 deg the near edge of the
  right bin fell just below the real image. After mounting, measure the
  camera pose and update the scene and URDF as `systems/so101/README.md`
  says; check the horizontal view from the published `camera_info`. Record
  and deploy with the real camera: simulated and real images still differ
  in rendering, lens distortion and exact pose. (The scene's camera used to
  be 640 x 480 at 69 deg, 54.5 deg vertically, at 45 deg; what that change
  means for policies trained on it: `applications/so101/README.md`,
  "Camera".)
- **Camera IMU.** `compositions/so101_hardware.yaml` launches the RealSense
  without its IMU (realsense-ros enables gyro and accelerometer only on
  request). Nothing in the policy path reads it; add
  `enable_gyro:=true enable_accel:=true unite_imu_method:=2` if a
  calibration tool needs it.
- **Motor driver.** `so101_hardware.yaml` expects a separate driver that
  publishes `/joint_states` (radians and metres, this repository's URDF
  convention) and subscribes `/so101/joint_commands`; the repository does
  not provide one. LeRobot's `so101_follower` (Feetech bus over the Waveshare
  board) reads and writes the servos; convert with
  `systems/so101/config/lerobot_joint_mapping.json` and check every joint's
  direction before arming the HIL guard (`SO101_HIL_ARMED=true`). The GR00T
  HIL profile uses `/so101/hardware/joint_states` and
  `/so101/hardware/joint_commands` by default: set
  `SO101_HARDWARE_STATE_TOPIC` and `SO101_HARDWARE_COMMAND_TOPIC` to match the
  driver.
- **Servo voltage.** The SO-101 uses 7.4 V STS3215 servos run from a 5 V
  supply (upstream: stall torque 16.5 kg cm at 6 V, slightly less at 5 V).
  The 12 V, 30 kg cm STS3215 is a drop-in for the follower only and needs a
  12 V 5 A or larger supply; the leader always uses 7.4 V servos.
- **Heights in the scene.** The scene (`systems/so101/scenarios/picking_table.json`)
  is the station: the workbench top at 0.400 m, the 31 mm riser
  (`arm_base_mount`, the printed `station_base_plate`) on it, the arm's base
  on the riser (spawn z 0.431 m), and the bins, objects and occluder on the
  table; each entry names what it rests on and the tests check every contact.
  So Isaac (which keeps objects where the scene puts them), Gazebo (which
  simulates them) and the physical station all start the task from the same
  state. (The scene used to have a 60 mm mount overlapping the arm's
  base by 29 mm and the objects and bins floating 60 and 32.5 mm above the
  table.)
- **USB.** Put the D435i on a USB 3 port of the host directly (it streams
  colour, depth and the aligned depth); the two Waveshare boards (USB
  serial) can share a powered hub.
- **Policy computer.** GR00T N1.7 needs an NVIDIA GPU with at least 16 GB of
  VRAM for inference alone (`applications/so101/README.md`); SmolVLA (450M
  parameters) is tested here on a 32 GB V100 and needs much less. The policy
  server can run on another machine (`SO101_GROOT_HOST`), so the station's
  own computer only needs Ubuntu 24.04, Docker and USB 3.

### Station

- 1 x **Intel RealSense D435i** (90 x 25 x 25 mm, 75 g; two M3 threads in
  the back, 3 mm deep at most) and its USB-C to USB-A 3 cable. A longer
  cable must be an active or good passive USB 3.1 Gen 1 cable, at most 2 m.
- 1 x **2020 aluminium extrusion, slot 6, 500 mm** (camera post; Misumi
  HFS5-2020 or any B-type 20-series profile).
- 7 x **M5 drop-in T-nut, slot 6** and 7 x M5 x 10 button head screw (head and
  foot to the post, three cable clips).
- 4 x **M4 x 25 socket head cap screw** and 4 x M4 brass heat-set insert
  (follower base to the riser).
- Camera cradle: 2 x M3 x 6 and 2 x M3 x 20 socket head, 2 x M3 nyloc nut,
  2 x M3 washer. Never longer than M3 x 6 into the camera.
- 1 x **station board**: 18 mm plywood or MDF, 1200 x 800 mm (or the
  workbench itself): the riser and the camera post foot are screwed to it, so
  the camera stays where it was calibrated when the station is moved; clamp
  the board to the table. Eight 4 mm pan-head wood screws (4 x 25 mm for the
  riser, 4 x 16 mm for the post foot).
- 1 x **calibration target**: AprilGrid tag36h11, 6 x 4 tags, 28 mm tags,
  8 mm spacing (Kalibr `tagSpacing` 0.2857), as in the scene; laser-print it
  at 100 % scale and glue it flat on a 280 x 200 mm rigid panel (3 mm
  aluminium composite, or 5 mm foam board). Check the tag size with calipers.
- Tape measure, double-sided tape pads (bins and occluder to the board),
  zip ties, Velcro straps, spiral cable wrap.
- 1 x control computer: Ubuntu 24.04, Docker, USB 3 (see Compatibility notes
  for the policy GPU); 1 x powered USB hub for the two arm boards.
- Spares: one of each servo ratio, 3-pin servo cables, a USB-C cable.

### Why these parts are bought, not printed

| Part | Why it stays purchased |
|---|---|
| Servos, servo boards, power supplies, camera | Electronics. |
| Camera post (2020 extrusion) | A 500 mm post does not fit the 250 mm bed in one piece, and a printed one would be several times more flexible: the camera must not shake when the arm moves the table. The aluminium post (I = 7,420 mm4) with the camera head on top is stiff enough that its first bending mode is far above the arm's motion. |
| Screws, heat-set inserts, T-nuts | Metal threads; printed threads strip and creep. Never more than 3 mm of thread into the camera. |
| Station board | Larger than the bed, and it must stay flat. |
| Calibration target | Its flatness and tag size set the calibration accuracy; a printed panel of 280 mm warps. Paper on a flat rigid panel is the standard. |
| Leader table clamps | Steel clamps; the leader is clamped exactly as upstream. |
| Cables | Electrical. |

### Assembly notes

- Build order, torques and checks: [parts/so101/README.md](parts/so101/README.md#steps).
- The riser is 31 mm, the scene's arm height above the table; the arm's x
  axis (toward the bins) is the riser's +x.
- Camera: the post foot's position puts the D435i's colour camera at the
  scene's pose (0.170, -0.522, 0.465 m from base_link) when the head is
  trimmed to the scene's 48.5 deg (the pitch the assembly check
  recommends); then measure the pose.
- Bins and objects: at their scene positions, standing on the board (see
  the README's task layout).

## Recommended assembly order

1. Print the servo gauges and check the fit, then print and build the
   follower arm ([Hugging Face SO-101 guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)).
2. Configure motor IDs and baud rate (`lerobot-setup-motors`) before routing
   cables permanently; calibrate both arms (`lerobot-calibrate`).
3. Screw the riser and the camera post foot to the station board, bolt the
   follower to the riser, mount the D435i and trim its pitch; leave room for
   the arm's full reach (the assembly check keeps the camera, head and post
   out of it).
4. Verify these ROS topics before adding higher-level policies:
   - `/joint_states`
   - `/so101/joint_commands`
   - `/so101/depth_camera/color/image_raw` and
     `/so101/depth_camera/aligned_depth_to_color/image_raw`
   - `/so101/depth_camera/color/camera_info`
5. Calibrate the camera intrinsics and the hand-eye/extrinsic pose (AprilGrid,
   or an AprilTag on the base) and update the scene camera and the URDF's
   `base_to_groot_camera` before collecting training data or evaluating
   grasping (`validate_calibration_and_dataset.py`).
